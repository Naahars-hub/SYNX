SYNX: Legal Metrology Compliance Checker

Complete Technical Architecture & Plain-English Explainer Guide
💡 The 10-Second Summary:
SYNX is an automated AI-powered packaging inspector. It photographs consumer goods (chips, cold drinks, biscuits), reads all text, measures letter heights in real-world millimeters, checks all 8 statutory requirements under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, and automatically generates a legally valid court certificate with fine calculations.

🔬 2. Step-by-Step Technical Breakdown

STEP 1
Smartphone Zero-Install Ingestion (REST API & QR Session)
The inspector clicks "Connect Phone Camera" on the laptop dashboard. A dynamic QR code pops up with an ephemeral session token (e.g., /mobile?session=934b5861d4). The inspector scans it with their phone camera. No mobile app download is needed. The phone web app allows capturing multiple angles (Front, Back, Cap) and uploads them to the server.
Plain English Analogy: Think of the REST API like a waiter in a restaurant. When you scan the QR code, the laptop creates a private mailbox with a secret key. Your phone drops photos into that mailbox, and the laptop checks the mailbox every second until the photos arrive.
STEP 2
Glare Attenuation & Adaptive Contrast (OpenCV + CLAHE)
Real-world FMCG packaging (chips bags, glossy pouches, shiny bottles) acts like a mirror under store lighting, creating bright white specular glare spots that blind standard OCR engines. We apply CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) and glare attenuation before text detection.
Plain English Analogy: It acts like polarized sunglasses for the AI. It cuts through the blinding light reflection on shiny plastic foil so you can clearly read faint, washed-out printing underneath.
STEP 3
Spatial Text Detection & Recognition (RapidOCR / PaddleOCR ONNX)
The preprocessed image is processed by a deep neural network model packed via ONNX runtime. It detects every text string and records its exact 4-corner bounding box coordinates (X, Y, Width, Height) along with a probability confidence score (e.g. 0.98).
Plain English Analogy: A compressed artificial brain that has learned what English letters look like. Because it runs on ONNX, it takes less than 1 second on a normal laptop CPU without needing an expensive gaming graphics card (GPU). The bounding boxes are like putting highlighter boxes over every word.
STEP 4
Physical Millimeter Calibration & Rule 7 PDP Calculations
Cameras only record digital pixels, but Indian law specifies mandatory font heights in millimeters (mm). We compute the physical scale factor (mm/pixel) using either the user-entered package dimensions or a reference object (like a ₹5 coin = 23mm). The system computes the Principal Display Panel (PDP) area (Rule 7: Area = W × H for boxes, or 40% × H × πD for cylindrical bottles) and looks up the statutory minimum numeral height tier (1.0mm, 2.0mm, 4.0mm, or 6.0mm).
Plain English Analogy: A digital ruler. By teaching the computer that "10 pixels on screen = 1 millimeter in real life", we measure the letters. If a letter is 1.8mm tall when the law requires ≥ 2.0mm, the system instantly flags it as an illegal violation!
STEP 5
NLP Entity Parsing & Blacklist Filtering
OCR provides a chaotic list of 60 raw text blocks. The Entity Parser acts as a legal detective to extract the 8 mandatory declarations:
  • Nutrition Blacklist: Filters out "Sugars: 14g" or "Energy: 100kJ" so they aren't mistaken for net weight or price.
  • MRP vs USP: Differentiates the total package price (Rs. 30.00) from per-unit rates (Rs. 0.075 / ml).
  • Dot-Matrix Dates: Extracts stamped inkjet dates (12/2026) from bottle necks/caps even without "Mfg Date:" labels.
  • FSSAI Disambiguation: Distinguishes 14-digit FSSAI license numbers from telephone consumer care helplines.
  • Address & PIN Code: Extracts factory address and verifies the mandatory 6-digit Indian PIN code.
Plain English Analogy: Finding the needles in the haystack. The OCR reads everything on the box; the parser figures out which text is the price, which is the factory, and which is just marketing fluff.
STEP 6
Multi-Angle 3D Product Aggregation
Real packaged goods are 3-dimensional. For example, on a cold drink bottle: brand is on the front label, factory is on the back panel, and price/batch/date is stamped on the neck. Our multi-angle aggregator tags each extraction with its origin (e.g. source_angle: "Angle 2 (Back)") and merges them into one complete statutory product profile.
Plain English Analogy: A 3D packaging jigsaw puzzle. It stitches multiple photos together into one unified brain so declarations on any side are credited to the product.
STEP 7
Decoupled Statutory Rulebook & Penalty Engine (Sec 36(1))
The assembled declarations are evaluated against legal_metrology_2011.json. Violations are mapped to statutory legal fines under Section 36(1) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (₹25,000 for 1st offence, ₹50,000 for 2nd offence, and up to ₹1,00,000 or 1 year imprisonment for subsequent offences).
Plain English Analogy: A living digital legal code. If Parliament updates a rule or raises fines tomorrow, administrators can edit the JSON file directly in the browser with live hot-reloading — zero lines of Python code need to be modified!
STEP 8
Court-Admissible PDF Certificate (SHA-256 Hashing)
Results render live on the HTML5 spatial canvas with interactive angle switcher tabs and color-coded bounding boxes. In the background, ReportLab compiles an official, signed PDF Compliance Audit Certificate complete with a SHA-256 cryptographic hash of the raw packaging image.
Plain English Analogy: A tamper-proof digital fingerprint. Under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, digital evidence must be authentic. If someone edits the photo by even 1 pixel in Photoshop, the hash breaks. This proves the audit certificate is 100% genuine in a court of law.

⚖️ 3. The 8 Mandatory Declarations at a Glance

Statutory Clause What It Means What SYNX Checks & Flags
Rule 6(1)(a) Manufacturer Details Extracts business name, complete address, and verifies 6-digit postal PIN code.
Rule 6(1)(b) Commodity Name Identifies genuine product title; filters out marketing slogans and process text.
Rule 6(1)(c) Net Quantity Enforces SI metric units (g, ml, kg); penalizes non-standard symbols (gms, kgs).
Rule 6(1)(d) Packing Date Extracts MM/YYYY or DD/MM/YYYY dates, including faint dot-matrix cap stamps.
Rule 6(1)(e) MRP & USP Extracts total retail price (Rs. 30.00) and separates Unit Sale Price (Rs. 0.075/ml).
Rule 6(1)(f) Customer Care Extracts phone helpline and email; rejects fake 14-digit FSSAI license numbers.
Rule 6(10) Country of Origin Verifies explicit declaration (Made in India) or manufacturer postal origin.
Rule 7 Tiers Font Height Calculates PDP area and verifies measured text height in millimeters.
